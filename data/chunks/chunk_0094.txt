5.8 Speed

Speeding is a major factor in many motor vehicle accidents. You
must obey the speed limits as specified on the roads or highways.
Driving faster than the posted speed limit on the road or highway
is illegal.

As a general rule you must not drive at a speed faster than it is
safe and reasonable under existing conditions on the road. Your
safe speed depends upon:

e Road conditions — curves, narrow bridges, wet and
slippery conditions, loose gravel, bumpy roads mean
driving at reduced speed.

e Visibility - darkness, rain, fog, smog, snow, intersections,
hills, curves can result in low visibility and call for reduced
speed.

e Traffic — Heavy road traffic means added dangers.

e Traffic Speed - Safe speed in a traffic condition is the
average speed at which the other traffic is moving provided that it
is not more than the posted speed. Travelling faster or slower
than other vehicles may increase the danger of an accident.

e Vehicle Condition — Condition of a vehicle including the
condition of tyres and braking system may also affect the control
you may have on the vehicle. For an older vehicle, with worn out
tyres and less than efficient braking system, it is advisable to
drive slowly.

e Your physical or mental condition — Your mental and
physical condition may also adversely affect your alertness while
driving resulting in less control on the vehicle.

The faster your speed is, lesser the time that you may have to
react and also requires longer distance to stop or slow down.
Travelling at faster speeds increases the likelihood of accidents.
And when an accident occurs at excessive speeds, the injuries
also tend to be more serious.

Speed Limit

Speed limit is the maximum speed at which you can drive a
vehicle under ideal condition. If the conditions are less than ideal,
you must slow down to a safe speed which is less than the speed
limit.
