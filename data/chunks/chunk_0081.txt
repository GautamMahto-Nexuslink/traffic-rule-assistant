5.2 Driving Checklist
Before Boarding Your Vehicle
e Wear comfortable clothes while driving. It is advisable to
wear shoes while driving, instead of slippers or chappals, to
have proper grip on the foot pedals.
e Do not drive if you are not feeling well mentally or
physically or are feeling tired and sleepy.
¢ Donot drive if you have taken medicine that can affect your
ability to safely control or drive a vehicle, or have
consumed alcoholic beverages. Drunken driving is an
offense under section 185 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988,
which is punishable up to six months of imprisonments or
fine up to Rs. 2000/- or both, in the first instance.
e Walk around your vehicle for any sign of problems like a flat
tyre, oil leak, open trunk etc.
Boarding Your Vehicle
e Know the inside of your vehicle to get comfortable with
controls and other features.
e Check that your view is not hampered due to dirty
windscreen or scratches thereon.
e Adjust your rear-view mirror and side mirrors for a good
view of the surroundings.

Check that you can comfortably reach the foot pedals. Push
the clutch pedal on the far left down to the floor. You should
be able to do this without stretching. If needed, adjust your
seat and steering wheel that will help you maintain a
comfortable posture.

Put on the seat belt and request all the passengers to do the
same. It is required by law that even the passengers sitting
on the front facing rear seats of the car should wear seat
belts, if provided.

Make sure that all the doors are properly locked, and

before you start the engine, check that the handbrake is on
and the gear lever is in neutral position for a manual
transmission vehicle. For automatic transmission vehicle,
the gear lever must be in'P' or'N' positions.

Starting Your Vehicle and Moving off

Check for any warning or alerts on the car's dashboard.
Normally, these warning signs go off once the engine
starts.

Use the proper turn signal, if needed, to merge into the
traffic.
